{\LARGE\bfseries SAGE-v2: Provably Budget-Consistent, Layer-Adaptive\\
KV-Cache Eviction for Memory-Efficient Long-Context Inference\par}
\vspace{1.1em}
{\normalsize Anonymous\par}
\vspace{0.4em}
{\small October 2026\par}
\end{center}
\vspace{0.6em}
\blfootnote{Author list withheld for double-blind review. Artifact: all code,
per-step accounting logs, and raw result files are released with this paper.
This work substantially revises and corrects an earlier, unpublished
manuscript on the same system; the corrections are documented in
Sections~\ref{sec:prelim}--\ref{sec:theory}.}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Eviction-based key--value (KV) cache compression lets large language models
serve long contexts under a memory contract, yet the layer-wise budget
formulas used by recent adaptive methods are heuristic: none of them
provably satisfies the global contract they advertise. We revisit this
problem from first principles. First, we show that a natural (and
previously published) entropy-exponent allocation formula violates the
global budget constraint at $97.4\%$ of decoding steps in simulation
(mean overshoot $+4.2\%$), because normalising dispersion ratios before
exponentiation does not preserve their mean. Second, we correct the
allocation with a \emph{capped water-filling projection} and prove that it
meets the global budget \emph{exactly} at every step, with per-layer floors
that protect attention sinks and recency, in $O(L\log B)$ time
(Theorem~\ref{thm:exact}). Third, we analyse the scoring side: an output
perturbation bound tied to evicted attention mass, an explicit staleness
separation between cumulative and decayed scores, and an equal-marginal
argument for dispersion-weighted budgets in a stylised tail model.
Fourth, we re-evaluate everything under a matched-entry protocol in which
every baseline (StreamingLLM, H2O, PyramidKV, and controls) is routed
through the same exact-budget projector on two architectures (GPT-2 with
multi-head attention; Qwen2.5-0.5B with grouped-query attention), replacing
the synthetic records of our earlier manuscript with fully traceable
measurements, and replicate the quality, retrieval, and sensitivity grids
on Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B-Instruct on GPU with the identical engine and protocol.
Experiments on WikiText-2 perplexity and needle retrieval with $3{,}840$
logged CPU budget checks plus a further $695{,}980$ GPU evaluation tokens
show zero contract violations, a
consistent advantage of dispersion-adaptive over static-pyramid allocation
that persists from $0.5$B to $3$B parameters, an existential role for sink
protection (removing it multiplies perplexity by $84\times$), and a
measurable quality--retrieval trade-off controlled by
the decay rate. All claims are calibrated to the scale actually measured,
and the released artifact regenerates every number from raw logs.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} KV cache compression; long-context inference;
layer-adaptive budget allocation; attention sinks; language models
