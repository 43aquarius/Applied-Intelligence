\section{Method: \sage{}}\label{sec:method}

\sage{} composes three mechanisms---\acs{} scoring, \laba{} allocation,
\dre{} protection---on top of a single exact-budget primitive. This
section defines each mechanism and the algorithm that ties them together;
Section~\ref{sec:theory} carries the guarantees.

\subsection{\acs: decayed cumulative scoring}

For layer $l$, let $\bar a_{t,l}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ge 0\,t}$ be the
attention distribution of the step-$t$ query averaged over query heads
(for grouped-query attention, every query head contributes to the average
of the KV entries it reads). \acs{} maintains an exponentially decayed
cumulative score, updated at every step for every retained entry $i$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:acs}
S_l[i] \;\leftarrow\; \gamma\,S_l[i] \;+\; \bar a_{t,l}[i],
\qquad \gamma\in(0,1],
\end{equation}
with new entries initialised by their received mass. $\gamma=1$ recovers
the cumulative scores of H2O~\cite{zhang2023h2o} and PyramidKV's
decode-time behaviour~\cite{cai2024pyramidkv}; $\gamma<1$ discounts stale
mass. Scores exist only for retained entries---we do not keep shadow
scores for evicted positions---so auxiliary memory stays at four bytes
per retained entry. Chunked processing (Section~\ref{sec:exp-setup})
applies the closed form
$S \leftarrow \gamma^{q} S + (\gamma^{\,q-1},\dots,1)\, A$ for a chunk of
$q$ queries with attention block $A$, which is algebraically identical to
$q$ single-step updates.

\subsection{\laba: dispersion-weighted exact allocation}

\laba{} turns a per-layer \emph{dispersion signal} into layer budgets
through the projection of Theorem~\ref{thm:exact}. The signal is the
normalised entropy of the recent head-averaged attention distribution,
exponentially smoothed across chunks:
\begin{align}
\kappa_l &\;\leftarrow\; \beta\,\kappa_l \;+\; (1-\beta)\,
\frac{H\!\bigl(\bar p_l\bigr)}{\log \lvert\mathcal{C}_l\rvert},
\qquad \beta = 0.9,
\end{align}
where $\bar p_l$ is the head-averaged attention mass of the last (up to)
16 queries of the current chunk and $H(\cdot)$ is Shannon entropy, so
$\kappa_l\in[0,1]$ measures how spread the layer's attention is. The
layer weights are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weights}
w_l \;\propto\; (\kappa_l + \varepsilon)^{\tau}, \qquad \tau\ge 0,
\end{equation}
normalised to sum to one, with defaults $\tau=1.5$ (the value used by our
earlier manuscript, retained deliberately so that the correction is
isolated from hyperparameter drift) and $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$. The
critical difference from the earlier formula is where normalisation
happens: the weights $w_l$ sum to one \emph{before} any budget is formed,
and the projection below allocates \emph{from} them, so no Jensen gap can
arise at any $\tau$. Given the weights, per-layer floors
$\ell_l=\min(p,t)$ (protection) and caps $u_l=t$, and the step budget
$B_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:contract}, the budget vector is
the unique capped water-filling allocation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:waterfill}
b_l \;=\; \operatorname{clip}\!\bigl(w_l\,\mu^{*},\ \ell_l,\ u_l\bigr),
\qquad \sum_l b_l = B_{\mathrm{eff}}(t),
\end{equation}
with the water level $\mu^{*}$ found by bisection
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:sage}). Setting $\tau=0$ recovers uniform allocation;
replacing Eq.~\eqref{eq:weights} with a static profile recovers
PyramidKV-style allocation---through the same projector, which is how the
matched-entry comparison instantiates its baselines.

\subsection{\dre: dual-region protection}

\dre{} never evicts two regions: the first $s=4$ positions (attention
sinks \cite{xiao2024streamingllm}) and the last $r=32$ positions
(recency). Protection is enforced structurally: the floors
$\ell_l=\min(s+r,t)$ in the projection guarantee that every layer can
always hold its protected region, and eviction only ranks the
\emph{middle} history by \acs{} scores, retaining the top-scoring
$b_l - (\text{protected})$ entries. When $b_l$ meets the floor exactly,
the layer keeps protection only. Structural protection has two
consequences we exploit in the evaluation: it makes the warmup allowance
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:contract} well-defined, and it removes the pathological
interactions that arise when a scoring policy is one tie away from
evicting a sink.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\SetAlFnt{\small}
\SetAlCapFnt{\small}
\SetAlCapNameFnt{\small}
\KwIn{weights $w$ (sum 1), floors $\ell$, caps $u$, budget
$B=\max(\rho L t,\,L\min(p,t))$, scores $S_l$, positions, cache}
\KwOut{updated cache with $\sum_l|\mathcal{C}_l|=B$ exactly}
\uIf{$B=\sum_l \ell_l$}{return $b\leftarrow\ell$\;}
\uElseIf{$B=\sum_l u_l$}{return $b\leftarrow u$\;}
Set $\mu_{\mathrm{lo}}\leftarrow0$,
$\mu_{\mathrm{hi}}\leftarrow\max_l u_l/w_l$\;
\While{$\mu_{\mathrm{hi}}-\mu_{\mathrm{lo}}>\varepsilon_\mu$}{
  $\mu\leftarrow(\mu_{\mathrm{lo}}+\mu_{\mathrm{hi}})/2$\;
  \If{$\sum_l \operatorname{clip}(w_l\mu,\ell_l,u_l) < B$}{
    $\mu_{\mathrm{lo}}\leftarrow\mu$ \; }{
    $\mu_{\mathrm{hi}}\leftarrow\mu$ \; }
}
$b\leftarrow$ integerise $\operatorname{clip}(w_l\mu_{\mathrm{hi}},\ell_l,u_l)$
by largest-remainder rounding inside the box; repair residuals $\pm1$
within slack\;
\For{layer $l=1,\dots,L$}{
  keep sinks $\cup$ recency $\cup$ top-$(b_l-{}$protected$)$ \acs{} scores
  among the middle region; index-select cache, positions, scores\;
}
\tcp{assert: $\sum_l|\mathcal{C}_l|=B$ and $\ell_l\le|\mathcal{C}_l|\le u_l$}
\caption{One compression step of \sage{} (\acs{}+$\laba$+\dre). The
bisection is the capped water-filling projection of
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact}; baselines replace the weights or the ranking
while keeping steps 1--7 identical. The assertion is executed in code,
not merely claimed: every run of Section~\ref{sec:exp} aborts on
violation.}
\label{alg:sage}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Complexity and implementation notes}

Each compression step costs $O(L\log B)$ for the bisection (each of the
$O(\log B)$ iterations evaluates the sum in $O(L)$), plus $O(\sum_l
|\mathcal{C}_l|\log|\mathcal{C}_l|)$ for per-layer top-$k$ selection when
selection is actually required. Measured on the 2-core CPU of
Section~\ref{sec:exp-setup}, scoring and compression together add
$4.4$--$9.6$\,ms per 64-token chunk on Qwen2.5-0.5B
(Table~\ref{tab:compliance}), against $200$--$500$\,ms of forward time
per chunk.

Two implementation decisions are integral to the honesty of our
evaluation. First, \emph{score provenance}: attention probabilities are
obtained from eager attention kernels, whose cost and memory are
explicitly accounted; we do not claim FlashAttention-compatible
zero-cost score extraction, which recent systems analyses identify as a
key deployment obstacle~\cite{mao2026infra,dao2023flashattention2}. At
GPU scale, chunked re-computation of scores over retained entries is the
drop-in replacement, and its cost is the same quantity our logs report.
Second, \emph{per-layer masking}: because budgets differ across layers,
cache lengths differ across layers after compression; we register a
custom attention kernel path that builds each layer's causal mask from
that layer's true key length, so retained entries in every layer are
attended exactly and evicted entries simply do not exist. This removes
the common workaround of padding all layers to a shared length, which
would itself violate the entry contract.
