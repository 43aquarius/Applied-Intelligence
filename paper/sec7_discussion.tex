\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:disc}

\paragraph{What the evidence supports.}
Four claims survive the matched-entry protocol. First, budget
consistency is achievable at negligible cost: the capped water-filling
projection held the contract exactly across every deployed step of every
configuration, while the exponent-style formula it replaces violated the
same contract in $97.4\%$ of simulated steps. Second, allocation
direction matters and static priors do not transfer: the pyramid prior
helped neither of our architectures relative to uniform budgets, and its
mirror image destroyed GPT-2 ($+39\%$ perplexity) while leaving Qwen
almost untouched. Third, structural protection of sinks and recency is
non-negotiable---the $84\times$ collapse without sink protection dwarfs
every allocation or scoring choice. Fourth, the decay rate is a real
dial, not a free lunch: it trades perplexity (best at $\gamma=1$) against
late-context retention and multi-instruction robustness (best at
$\gamma<1$), and Proposition~\ref{prop:staleness} explains the mechanism.
Fifth, every ordering above survives the extension to $8$K and $16$K
contexts on the 1.5B model, where the same family leads at every window
measured ($8.661$/$8.373$), the retention advantage of decayed scoring
widens to $6$--$11\times$ H2O, and the chunked online protocol is not
merely an implementation choice but what makes the depth executable on
the replication hardware at all
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-ext8k}).

\paragraph{What it does not support.}
We report two negative results with the same prominence as the positive
ones. Dispersion-adaptive allocation did not beat uniform allocation on
the GQA model at this scale (16.40 uniform versus 17.02 adaptive at
$\rho=0.2$, although adaptive still dominates the static pyramid by 1.25
points); and default-rate decay cost 1--2 perplexity points on both
models. Both results bound the strength of our claims deliberately: the
case for \laba{} is that it never loses to the static prior on either
architecture and wins clearly on one, not that it dominates everywhere.
Likewise, the needle results are a measured \emph{failure} of the entire
history-only family at $20\%$ budget, ours included; we consider the
retention diagnostic that localises the failure (cache content, not
generation) more useful than a leaderboard in which every score-based
method scores zero at mid depths. The deep-window extension adds a third
calibration, on ourselves: the matched-entry floor is achieved up to
cache availability, and under the adaptive dial deep-window budget jumps
can transiently outrun the fixed refill rate (4 of 128 steps at $8$K,
57 of 256 at $16$K, all one-sided under-retention, bounded and
self-correcting; Section~\ref{sec:exp-ext8k}). The projection itself is
exact everywhere---every such deficit is a keep-all branch, not an
allocation error---and the uniform-budget variant and every static
baseline are floor-exact at every step measured.

\paragraph{Choosing the dials in practice.}
The sensitivity grids double as a selection guide, and the honest summary
is architecture- and workload-dependent. For decay $\gamma$: start from
$\gamma=1$ on stationary language-modelling text, where long memory is
rewarded and the grid is monotone---over-decay, not under-decay, is the
main risk ($54.50$ at $\gamma=0.98$ versus $46.89$ at $\gamma=1$ on
GPT-2; Table~\ref{tab:sens}); introduce gentle decay
($\gamma\in[0.99,0.995]$) when scores must forget stale mass, which is
what the GQA family prefers in our runs ($17.02$ with decay versus
$17.43$ without at $0.5$B; $10.45$ versus $10.65$ at $1.5$B). A cheap
rule: probe $\gamma\in\{1,0.999,0.995,0.99\}$ on a 16-sequence
perplexity slice at the target $\rho$ and pick the largest $\gamma$
within one perplexity point of the minimum---the probe runs in minutes
on CPU, so the dial costs almost nothing to set. For the exponent $\tau$
(the budget profile): the measured dispersion profile, not a prior,
should arbitrate---GPT-2's non-monotone profile rewards stronger
weighting ($\tau=2.0$ is best in its grid) while every Qwen scale and
window we tested is best served by the uniform endpoint ($\tau=0$:
$16.40$ at $0.5$B; $10.30/9.19$ at $1.5$B/3B in the GPU replication;
$8.66/8.37$ at $8$K/$16$K), so probe
$\tau\in\{0,1.5,2\}$ and fall back to uniform when the profile is flat
or the probe is inconclusive. The $8$K/$16$K extension adds a
deployment-grade tiebreaker to that fallback: the uniform endpoint is
floor-exact at every depth, while the adaptive dial's deep-window
budget jumps can transiently outrun the refill rate
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-ext8k}). Both dials are two constants in the
released engine; no retraining is involved.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The core study is CPU-scale by construction (models to $0.5$B parameters,
contexts to $4{,}096$ tokens, single-sequence execution at
$\sim$61 tokens/s prefill on the larger model), and the GPU replication
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-gpu}) extends the quality, retrieval, and
sensitivity grids to Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B on T4 hardware; the extension
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-ext8k}) pushes the quality and retrieval grids to
$8$K/$16$K contexts on the 1.5B model. Not beyond, however: 7B-class
models and 32K+ contexts remain unmeasured, the 3B model does not run
at $8$K under the fp32 matched-entry protocol on a $16$\,GB T4 (the
feasibility probe exhausted memory $24$\,MiB short---a genuine capacity
ceiling for this protocol/hardware combination), and reaching $16$K at
all depends on the chunked online path, since a monolithic full-window
attention pass needs $12$\,GiB at that depth and exceeds the device
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-ext8k}); throughput under
batching, kernel fusion, or paged allocators remains unmeasured, and our
GPU overheads are wall-clock in eager mode rather than profiled kernels;
the systems interactions that dominate at production scale
\cite{kwon2023paged,mao2026infra} are outside what this hardware can
measure. Perplexity uses $10$--$32$ evaluation windows (dictated by
compute), which suffices for the effect sizes reported but is smaller
than the full-test-set convention; the needle grid uses two trials per
depth at 2{,}048 and one at 4{,}096 tokens, and one trial per depth
position (five depths at $8$K, three at $16$K) in the extension. Our
baseline set covers the
eviction family and its layer-allocation axis; we do not compare against
query-aware methods (SnapKV, Quest, DuoAttention) or quantisation (KIVI)
because they change the deployment model rather than the eviction policy,
and the matched-entry protocol we advocate is exactly the instrument
under which such comparisons should be run once hardware permits. The
earlier manuscript's synthetic records are fully superseded: no number in
this paper descends from them.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The artifact contains the engine, all policies, the projector with its
assertion harness, every result JSON and accounting log behind every
table and figure---CPU and GPU alike---the scripts that regenerate them,
the GPU drivers that reproduced the 1.5B/3B grids and the $8$K/$16$K
extension unmodified on T4 hardware with their feasibility-probe
records, and the replay harness that re-derives the deep-window
floor-deficit mechanism without a model. A single command re-derives
each table from the logs; a single
flag re-runs any configuration with a different seed. We believe this is
the appropriate response to the review context that motivated this work:
claims about memory contracts should be checkable at the level of
individual compression steps, not only through downstream benchmarks.
